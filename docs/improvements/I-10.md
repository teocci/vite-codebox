# I-10 — Derive the camera planes and grid step from the world extent

- **Improvement ID:** I-10
- **Version:** 0.7.0
- **Date:** 2026-07-27
- **Status:** ✅ DONE (483 tests; live-verified).
- **Related work:** completes I-8's scale gate — prerequisite for the deferred runtime extent-grow op

## Objective

Make config.yaml's extent a knob that actually works, so raising it is a supported answer rather than a suggestion that breaks the render.

## Approach

I-8's scale gate answers an oversized subject by naming the `world.extent` that would hold it at 1:1.
Three literals in the viewer, all tuned for the 64 m world that shipped, made that answer false past
about a kilometre — and a fourth value, `gridStep`, made the floor unreadable at the same scale.

The fix is that none of the four is a constant: each derives from `world.extent`, in
`packages/shared/config.js`, as a pure function of the extent rather than a getter reading config.
That distinction is what makes them testable without the suite depending on whichever number
config.yaml happens to carry — the tests exercise seven extents from 1 m to 5000 m, and the whole
suite passes identically at `extent: 32` and `extent: 1400`.

**The far plane** was the defect: at 5000 m a 2737 m subject sits at a fit distance of ~4224 m with
its far corner at ~5597 m, and clipped. Rather than guess a bigger number, the derivation makes the
guarantee provable — an explicit orbit cap (`maxOrbit`, 12 extents) is handed to OrbitControls as
`maxDistance`, and the far plane is that cap plus the buildable box's half-diagonal (√3·extent) with
a 5% margin. Nothing can be further from the camera than a distance the camera is not allowed to
reach, so "the world never clips away" stops being generosity and becomes arithmetic.

**The near plane** was a trade the derivation removes rather than rebalances. A near that tracks far
at a fixed depth ratio has to be clamped at both ends, and every clamp is a scale at which it is
wrong. Enabling a logarithmic depth buffer in the renderer spends depth precision evenly across the
range instead of hoarding it near the eye, which lets near and far be chosen independently — so near
is simply `BLOCK_SIZE`, the finest thing that can exist, at every extent.

**The grid step** is a legibility problem, not a geometry one: what matters is the cell *count*, not
the cell size. `gridStepFor` picks the smallest 1-2-5 ladder rung that holds the grid at or under 64
divisions, so the number stays round — a derived 43.75 m cell is legible to nobody, 50 m is. The
ladder returns exactly 1 m at `extent: 32`, so the shipped world's grid is unchanged. `gridStep` in
config.yaml now accepts `auto` (the default) and still honours a pinned number.

`extent` itself stays at 32. Raising it is now supported, but shipping the maximum as the default
would put every ordinary build — a 5 m car, a 51 m house — on a 50 m grid inside a 2.8 km floor,
serving a case nobody builds by default at the cost of the case everyone does. The scale gate's
advice is the trigger; the default is the common case.

## Files changed

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `packages/shared/config.js` | New pure derivations `gridStepFor`, `cameraPlanesFor`, `cameraStartFor`, `resolveGridStep`; `WORLD` gains `nearPlane`, `farPlane`, `maxOrbit`, `cameraStart`, and `gridStep` becomes a resolving getter |
| `apps/web/src/viewer/Viewer.js` | Camera planes and opening position read from `WORLD` instead of `0.1`/`5000`/`(42,32,54)`; renderer gets `logarithmicDepthBuffer: true` |
| `apps/web/src/viewer/CameraDirector.js` | `controls.maxDistance = WORLD.maxOrbit` — the cap the far plane is derived from |
| `scripts/gen-config.mjs` | `gridStep` defaults to `'auto'` rather than `1` |
| `config.yaml` | `gridStep: auto`; extent comment states that raising it is supported and names 1400 for a 1:1 Golden Gate |
| `tests/shared.test.js` | 17 tests across the four derivations; the pre-existing out-of-bounds test rederived from `WORLD.boundBlocks` |

## Verification

483 tests green — 91 vitest (17 new, each written test-first and watched fail), 112 Go unit, 24 Go
e2e, 256 pytest. The suite passes identically at `extent: 32` and `extent: 1400`.

The tests assert invariants rather than restate the formulas: that the far plane exceeds the orbit
cap plus the world's half-diagonal at every extent; that a kilometre world's far plane clears the
5000 that clipped it; that the near plane is one block; that the grid holds 25–70 divisions across
four decades of extent; and that `extent: 32` still yields exactly the 1 m cell and the `(42, 32, 54)`
opening the viewer always had.

One pre-existing test was extent-coupled in the same way the viewer was — it asserted a literal
4000-block box was out of bounds, true only at `extent: 32`. It now derives the magnitude from
`WORLD.boundBlocks`.

Live-verified at `extent: 1400` by rebuilding `builds/golden-gate-bridge.json` at true 1:1 — 2737 m
end to end, 1280 m main span, 227 m towers, 143 m cable sag, 386 parts. Both anchorages and the full
deck render unclipped where the old far plane would have cut roughly 600 m from each end; the derived
50 m grid reads at 56 divisions instead of 2800; and there is no z-fighting where 256 cable segments
cross the towers, which is what the logarithmic depth buffer is there to prevent.
