## Objective

Leave in prose only what needs judgment, and make the remaining examples true at 1:1.

## Approach

Two halves: prose that was actively wrong, and generators that should have existed before the prose ever needed to describe them.

**The examples taught the error.** `SKILL.md`'s castle was `size [40,14,40]` — at 2 cm blocks, an 80 cm castle — and its proportion advice recommended "a deck at 8 blocks and a 40-block span", a 16 cm deck. Neither said "a block is a metre", but both demonstrated it, and a worked example outranks a caveat. With P-9's gate in place the cost of leaving them changed in kind: the documentation now led the model into a refusal it could not have predicted from the documentation. The castle is replaced by a 6 m pavilion carrying `subject.mm` and built from the new generators, and every documented command in the file was executed before being written down — an example that does not run is worse than none.

The metre value deliberately stays out of the prose. The file's own rule is that runtime values come from the server, so it points at `world.py`'s `blocksPerMetre` and `dims.py to-blocks` rather than printing a number that `config.yaml` can change.

**The cost model was false, and measurably so.** "One `box` of 40x1x6 is one object; forty 1x1x6 boxes are forty… one is 40x the cost" is wrong twice. `World.addPart` keys layers as `${kind}:${family}` where family is the *render family*, and per-instance colour comes from `setColorAt` — so forty boxes of forty different opaque materials share one `InstancedMesh` and are **one draw call**. With five `GEOMETRY_KINDS` and four families, the entire world is bounded at twenty draw calls at any part count. What part count does cost is an instance slot and a matrix write, and `InstancedLayer` sets `frustumCulled = false`, so every instance is submitted each frame whether on screen or not — cost tracks the total, not the visible subset. There is no protocol ceiling either: F-1 set `SetReadLimit(-1)`. Budgets are therefore stated as legibility guidance, not as an engine limit, and the advice inverts — curved and raked forms need many parts, and the old guidance steered away from exactly the shapes the new generators produce.

**Five generators, because prose was standing in for arithmetic.** `SKILL.md` told the model to compose openings around a gap without giving it anything to compute the gap with, which is the one thing the skill's own division of labour forbids.

- `wheel` — a `tube` with an optional hub, the shape P-8 was added for. The hub stands *proud* of the tyre rather than flush; flush, it is invisible from every angle but dead-on and the part is spent for nothing.
- `taper` — slabs narrowing base to top: spires, hulls, chimneys, none expressible with `shell`. Heights are distributed by integer division rather than by rounding each boundary, which guarantees what rounding does not — no zero-height slab, and consecutive slabs sharing a face exactly.
- `dome` — one `ellipsoid` of twice the rise, half of it inside what it rests on, since a hemisphere cannot be cut from a sphere. It refuses a base that cannot clear its rise rather than letting the bounds gate report a `y` the caller never wrote.
- `pane` — raked glazing. This one is forced by the renderer: `World._compose` composes every instance with `IDENTITY_QUAT`, so nothing is ever rotated and a leaning surface must be a staircase of thin slabs. The evidence for it is the rebuilt `builds/tesla-model-s.json`, whose `greenhouse` stage spends **178 of the build's 305 parts** stepping that shape out by hand.
- `window` — a wall composed around an opening. A piece that would come out zero-sized is omitted rather than emitted, which is what makes a door (sill on the floor) and a shopfront (opening the full width) the same generator as a window instead of three special cases.

Two decisions in `pane` are worth recording because both were driven by consequences elsewhere. Its frame is **inset into the glazing**, not added around it, so framing a pane cannot change its envelope — otherwise a cosmetic choice would grow the build past its declared subject and P-9's gate would reject the plan for it. And the frame **caps both ends**, closing all four sides: framing only the long rails was shipped first, looked wrong in the viewer immediately (two loose stepped strips with bare glass between them), and was corrected. A cap replaces its step's glazing rather than sitting on top of it, so capping costs fewer parts, not more, and still leaves the envelope alone.

`shapes.py` now imports `world` for `AXES` instead of keeping a second copy of the axis names, which makes its docstring's claim that anchoring "lives in world.py and is imported, never restated" true for the first time.

## Verification

`pytest` — 256 passed, 1 skipped for `codeblox-builder` (was 233 after the first three generators, 210 before the phase). `npm test` — 74 passed, unchanged; no JavaScript was touched.

Every command in `SKILL.md` was run before it was documented: all nine generator invocations and both `dims.py to-blocks` forms. That check earned its keep — the `pane` example without `--steps` emits **116 parts**, because the default is one slab per block of rise, which is a third of a vehicle's stated budget for one windshield. The documented example now sets `--steps` and the file explains the dial rather than leaving it to be discovered.

Live-verified twice against a running server. A `rotunda` exercising `wheel`, `taper` and `dome` (8 parts, extent matching its declared subject exactly), and a `shelter` exercising `pane` and `window`. The pavilion from `SKILL.md` was also built before being written into the file: 27 parts in 5 stages.

The registry guard did its job mechanically: adding the subparsers broke `test_every_generator_shapes_exposes_is_reachable_from_a_plan` until `build.SHAPES` was updated — the generators were reachable from the CLI and invisible to a plan, and nothing else would have reported it.

Two defects were caught that tests alone would not have. `pane` first divided the rise by `count` instead of `count - 1`, so it climbed one step short of the rise it was given — invisible in a screenshot, wrong against a declared subject. And the missing end caps were found by looking at the render.

One thing the live pass demonstrated by accident: the first `shelter.json` declared 3 m tall while its canopy topped out at 194 blocks, and P-9's gate refused it with `spread 23%, over the 15% limit` and `the y axis is the outlier`. The declaration was wrong, not the geometry — which is the confusion the ratio triple exists to separate, working on a plan written after it.
